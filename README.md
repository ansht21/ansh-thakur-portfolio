## 🎯 Business Questions I Answered

If an interviewer asks one of these, the query and the number are already in my [Data Analytics repository](https://gitlab.com/ansht_121/data-analytics).

| Business Question | Finding | Decision It Drives |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Sales have been flat for the last 6 months — why?** | **Revenue is flat, not down — but profit fell 4.09%.** Net revenue **+0.50%** (₹1.41 cr) while profit dropped **₹1.69 lakh** and margin fell **134 bps** (29.44% → 28.10%). Orders **+4.94%**, units **+4.84%** — demand *grew*. The gap is **price**: ASP fell **4.14%**, and splitting it shows **−3.97 pp is gross price**, only **−0.18 pp** is discount. | **Don't launch a demand campaign** — it treats a symptom that doesn't exist. Fix price realisation: review Distributor (−4.17 pp margin) and Online (−1.67 pp) first |
| **Are we exposed to losing one big customer? Should we build a key-account programme?** | **No — there is no key account to protect.** The largest of 50 customers is **2.75%** of net revenue, the top 5 are **12.35%**, and the Herfindahl index of **203** implies **49.3 effective customers of 50**. The top store of 367 is **0.6%**. | **Declined to build a key-account programme** and put the effort into pricing, where the money actually leaks. Concentration analysis exists to *rule risks out*, not just find them |
| **Which sales team is actually costing us margin — pricing or discounting?** | **A 5.03 pp margin spread across 28 teams, and it is a discount-behaviour gap.** Best team **31.55%** margin at 11.11% average discount vs worst team **26.52%** at 11.63%. Margin correlates **−0.638** with average discount but only **+0.103** with ASP, and order counts are near-equal (1.4×) — so it is not a mix effect. | **Gated discount approvals for the bottom-5 teams** (9, 18, 1, 2, 12) instead of a generic sales incentive. Bringing them to the median margin adds **₹2,45,957 (1.16% of total profit)** |
| **Is the evening revenue peak a real surge-pricing effect, or just more trips?** | **A genuine price premium — and it survives the control.** Peak hours earn **₹26.25/km vs ₹19.39/km** (+6.86). Airport share is near-identical in peak vs quiet hours (**39.96% vs 39.61%**), and on non-airport trips alone the premium is **₹27.23 vs ₹20.02** — **105% of the headline effect survives** the confounder check. | **Recommended pricing the peak window explicitly** (~35% per km currently captured implicitly) instead of assuming the volume peak is the whole story |
| **Is churn a price problem — do customers leave because they pay more?** | **No — and the relationship is not even monotonic.** Churn by monthly-charge quartile is **11.32% → 24.59% → 37.55% → 32.88%**. The *most expensive* quartile churns **4.67 pp less** than the third. | **Rejected blanket discounting to retain customers** — it would have added cost without saving revenue. Redirected to contract structure and onboarding |
| **Is paperless billing a real churn driver, or just a proxy for contract type and payment method?** | **Real — it survives a triple control.** Uncontrolled, paperless churns **33.59% vs 16.38%**, but it is also over-represented on month-to-month plans and electronic-check accounts. Holding contract, *tenure and payment method* constant at once, paperless still churns worse in **31 of 38 cells** (mean lift **+8.30 pp**; month-to-month / 0–12 mo / electronic check: **67.8% vs 50.2%**). The effect holds *within* every single payment method. | **Reframed paperless billing as a product defect to fix** — first-payment failures, card expiry, refund friction — not a segment to target. A cheap fix the churn-rate table would have hidden |
| Is deep discounting the real cause — and was late delivery to blame? | **Discounting is a real but partial cause; delivery is not.** Revenue in the 20%+ band grew **13.03% → 16.79%** of net revenue; reverting the mix would have held margin at **28.62%** instead of 28.10% — the shift alone cost **52 bps ≈ ₹73,617**. Meanwhile on-time delivery went **49.4% → 52.3% → 48.5%** and late-revenue exposure stayed flat at ~50%. | Set a **product-level profit floor**, not a blanket discount cap (the 5–10% band is healthy at ~33% margin). Keep delivery on a **separate workstream** |
| Half our orders are late — which warehouse is at fault? | **None.** Only **0.08%** of delivery-time variance is between warehouses; all six have 2.82–2.88 day spread. | Fix upstream scheduling, not a facility — a per-site project would fix nothing |
| Isn't our biggest warehouse the worst offender? | **No — a volume illusion.** It causes 31.9% of late orders but handles 31.4% of all orders. | Always normalise counts by volume before ranking anything |
| Are discounts eating our margin? Should we cap them? | **Yes — at high levels.** Orders at 20%+ discount return only **4.64%** margin versus 31.02% in the 5–10% band, with 373 orders unprofitable. | Set product-level profit floors and review the 20%+ discount band |
| When should a ride-hailing firm surge-price? | **5–7 PM carries 31.2% of demand** in 3 of 24 hours — and the surge *starts* at 5 PM, not 6 PM when volume peaks. | Launch driver incentives by 4:30 PM, an hour ahead of peak |
| Do higher-rated drivers earn more per trip? | **No, and the obvious read is backwards.** Low-rated drivers show higher fares — but only because they drive longer trips. Fare-per-km is flat at ₹22.06–22.41. | Investigate long-trip rider experience; don't penalise drivers |
| Can we focus on just our top revenue zones? | **No.** It takes **4 of 5 zones** to reach 80% of revenue. | Keep coverage city-wide — the 80/20 assumption fails here |

**Fourteen of these; eight say "don't act."** Knowing where *not* to spend is the part that saves budget.

## 📊 Featured Data Analytics Projects

### 1. 📦 [Supply Chain Performance Analysis](https://gitlab.com/ansht_121/data-analytics/-/tree/main/supply-chain-performance-analysis)

![Supply Chain Dashboard](images/supplychain-dashboard.webp)

![Supply Chain Heatmap](images/supplychain-heatmap.webp)

*An end-to-end operational analytics project featuring a Python data cleaning pipeline, custom SQL metrics, and an automated, fully interactive 10-chart Excel dashboard.*

* **Business Challenge:** Half of all orders miss the 5-day delivery target, with ₹3.63 cr of net revenue exposed — and leadership needs to know whether to invest in a specific warehouse or the network.
* **Tech Stack:** Python (Pandas, openpyxl), SQL (MySQL — CTEs, `STDDEV`, `LAG`, window functions), Statistical Variance Decomposition (ANOVA principles), Microsoft Excel (VBA, Macros, Pivot Tables).
* **Key Achievements:**
  * **Disproved the obvious answer.** Ranking warehouses by late-order count blames the largest site; variance decomposition shows only **0.08%** of delivery-time variance is between warehouses. Prevented a misdirected improvement programme.
  * **Root-caused a revenue stall.** A volume/price/mix decomposition showed revenue is **flat (+0.50%)**, not down, while profit fell **4.09%** — orders grew **4.94%** but ASP fell **4.14%**. A SKU-level reconciliation proved **price**, not volume or assortment, caused the plateau, and a discount-band counterfactual priced the mix shift at **52 bps ≈ ₹73,617**.
  * **Ruled out a risk that wasn't there.** Revenue concentration analysis (Herfindahl index **203** → **49.3 effective customers of 50**) proved no key-account programme was needed, and showed the **5.03 pp** sales-team margin spread is a **discount-behaviour** gap (margin correlates **−0.638** with discount, only **+0.103** with ASP), not a pricing-ability gap.
  * Cleaned and processed raw transactional data, generating dynamic metrics like **Delivery Days**, **Order-to-Ship Days**, and **Profit Margins** (₹7.30 cr net revenue / ₹2.12 cr profit across 7,991 orders).
  * Implemented an automated Excel workbook generator using `openpyxl` with dynamic dropdown selectors, date-range inputs, and custom formula-driven pivot tables.
  * Used **ABC Classification** to segment the 47-SKU catalogue by net-revenue contribution (A-items = 80.6% of net revenue) — with an honest read that this catalogue shows only moderate, not textbook 80/20, concentration.
  * Shipped a reproducible Python pipeline so every quoted figure regenerates from the raw data.

### 2. 🚖 [Uber Trip Demand Analysis](https://gitlab.com/ansht_121/data-analytics/-/tree/main/uber-trip-demand-analysis)

![Uber Power BI Dashboard](images/uber-dashboard.webp)

![Uber Demand Heatmap](images/uber-heatmap.webp)

*A comprehensive revenue and behavioral analysis of ride-sharing transactions using SQL data warehousing, Python visualization, and Excel dashboarding.*

* **Business Challenge:** Identifying when to surge-price, where to position premium vehicles, and which fleet tier to grow across 35,000 trips.
* **Tech Stack:** MySQL (window functions, CTEs, `LAG`, running totals), Python (Seaborn, Matplotlib), Advanced Excel, Power BI.
* **Key Achievements:**
  * Used `LAG()` to separate peak *volume* from peak *growth* — demand accelerates at **5 PM (+138%)**, an hour before the 6 PM volume peak, moving the incentive trigger an hour earlier.
  * Caught a confounded metric: lower-rated drivers appear to earn more, but the effect vanishes once trip distance is controlled for (**fare-per-km flat at ₹22.06–22.41**).
  * Quantified that **airport trips are 39.6% of volume but 54.8% of revenue** (₹535.77 vs ₹289.40 avg fare).
  * **Proved the evening peak is a real price premium, not a mix artefact.** Peak hours earn **₹26.25/km vs ₹19.39/km**; with airport trips removed the premium is *larger* (**₹27.23 vs ₹20.02**) — **105% survives** the confounder check — so the ~35% per-km peak premium is being captured only implicitly.
  * Built a MySQL schema over 35,000 records with `ROW_NUMBER()` deduplication and null validation.
  * Conducted vehicle-category analysis measuring revenue per ride and revenue-to-distance efficiency (Uber Black ₹40.06/km vs Uber Pool ₹14.08/km).
  * Reported three **negative findings** (no weekend fare effect, no seasonality, no zone concentration) that removed unjustified recommendations, and showed driver concentration is a non-risk (HHI **100.3** → **99.7 effective drivers**).

### 3. 📊 [Subscription Retention Analytics](https://gitlab.com/ansht_121/data-analytics/-/tree/main/subscription-retention-analytics)

![Churn Dashboard](images/retention-model.png)

*End-to-end churn risk and revenue-at-risk analysis on 7,032 customers, delivered through a custom Python pipeline, SQL cohort analysis, and an interactive Excel dashboard.*

* **Business Challenge:** A subscription business is losing ~26% of customers. Which customers leave, when, why, and what revenue is exposed — so retention spend can be prioritised by dollars saved, not by gut feel.
* **Tech Stack:** Python (Pandas, Seaborn, openpyxl), SQL (MySQL — `LAG`, `RANK`, `STDDEV_SAMP`, CTEs), Microsoft Excel (native charts, filterable table), Jupyter.
* **Key Achievements:**
  * Identified **Month-to-month contracts churn at 42.7%** versus near-zero on two-year plans (chi-square = 1179.5, Cramér's V 0.41) and quantified that **month-to-month + fiber-optic = 72% of revenue at risk**.
  * **Killed a false price story and proved a real billing driver.** Churn by charge quartile is **non-monotonic** (11.32% → 24.59% → 37.55% → 32.88%) — the most expensive quartile churns *less* — while paperless billing survives a contract × tenure × payment-method triple control (worse in **31 of 38 cells**, mean lift **+8.30 pp**). Priced a 1 pp churn reduction at **$4,557/month**.
  * Built a 5-KPI, 6-chart executive Excel dashboard with a left-side Filters panel matching the supply-chain reference style — native, filterable, formula-driven from the ChartData sheet.
  * Caught a confounder: fiber-optic customers churn more, but a large part of the effect is the contract mix, not the service itself — the dashboard splits the two.
  * **Corrected three claims with stronger evidence.** Seniors are *not* immaterial (41.68% vs 23.65%, surviving contract and paperless controls), partner/dependents effects survive the contract control (dependents churn *less*: 15.53% vs 31.28%), and paperless billing *is* an independent driver — it survives a contract × tenure × payment-method triple control (31 of 38 cells, +8.30 pp). The old "negative findings" were under-tested; the new analysis tests each against its confounders.
