Case Study 01

Supply Chain Performance Analysis

1 Business Problem

Half of all orders miss the 5-day delivery target, with $41M of revenue exposed. The leadership asks: is one bad warehouse causing this — or is the lateness network-wide? A misdirected per-site improvement project would burn budget on the wrong fix.

2 Data

7,991 transactional orders across 2018–2020, 47 SKUs, six warehouses. Raw CSV cleaned in Python; engineered metrics: delivery_days, order-to-ship_days, and profit_margin. Loaded into a typed MySQL schema for analytical querying.

3 Analysis

Ranked warehouses by late-order count, then decomposed delivery-time variance between sites vs. within sites. Ran ABC classification across the 47-SKU catalogue and a 32-month revenue / margin stability audit.

4 Key Findings

0.08%
delivery-time variance between warehouses
37.4%
avg. profit margin, all SKUs
78.7%
revenue from A-tier SKUs (ABC)
₹0
loss-making orders (pricing floors hold)
+1.0%
2020 YoY growth stall (flagged)

The largest warehouse tops raw late-order count because it handles 31.4% of all volume — not because it is slow. All six warehouses show an equivalent 2.82–2.88 day spread. A per-site project would have fixed nothing.

5 Business Recommendation

Do not run a per-warehouse improvement programme — lateness is systemic and upstream (scheduling / dispatch), not localisable to one site. Instead, investigate the upstream order-to-ship process. Separately, the discount policy needs no cap (margin is stable at ~37% across discount bands) and a 2020 growth stall worth escalation.

1 Business Problem

When should a ride-hailing operator surge-price, where should premium vehicles be positioned, and which fleet tier deserves growth investment — across 35,000 real trip records.

2 Data

35,000 trip records with fares, distances, vehicle categories, pickup zones, and timestamps. Built a two-stage MySQL cleaning pipeline: DISTINCT for exact duplicates then ROW_NUMBER() partitioned by Trip_ID for logic-based deduplication, plus a null integrity audit.

3 Analysis

Used LAG() to separate peak volume from peak growth. Decomposed revenue per kilometre across vehicle categories and pickup zones. Quantified the airport vs. city-trip fare premium.

4 Key Findings

31.2%
demand in 5–7 PM (3 of 24 hrs)
5 PM
demand accelerates, +138% over 4 PM
54.8%
airport revenue from 39.6% of trips
2.8×
Uber Black vs Pool per km

Three of the eight findings marked “don’t act”: no weekend fare effect (0.9% gap), no seasonal cycle (11% month variance), and no valid “top zones” strategy — 4 of 5 zones are needed to reach 80% of revenue.

5 Business Recommendation

Do launch surge and driver incentives for 5–7 PM by 4:30 PM (an hour ahead of the 5 PM growth start), protect airport supply (54.8% of revenue from 39.6% of trips), and grow premium vehicles that earn 2.8× more per km. Do not spend on weekend fare tweaks, seasonality campaigns, or zone concentration — three levers the data shows are ineffective.

08 · Process

Analytics Workflow

The seven-step process behind every project on this page.

  1. 1

    Business Problem

    Define the specific question the business needs answered. Never start with the dataset.

  2. 2

    Data Inspection

    Load raw data, audit nulls, check data types, understand what each field actually means.

  3. 3

    Cleaning & Validation

    Remove duplicates, handle missing values, standardise formats, run data-quality checks.

  4. 4

    SQL / Python Analysis

    Aggregations, window functions, CTEs, and Python EDA to surface patterns and exceptions.

  5. 5

    Visualization

    Power BI or Excel dashboards that communicate findings to non-technical stakeholders.

  6. 6

    KPI Interpretation

    Distinguish reliable findings from noise — and report negative results honestly.

  7. 7

    Business Recommendation

    Deliver clear, evidence-backed actions — including where the data says not to act.
