Flagship Case Studies

Three end-to-end analytical solutions.

1. Supply Chain Inventory & Performance Optimization

Disproved a multi-million-dollar warehouse overhaul by proving late deliveries were systemic, and identified an executive pricing floor to prevent discount margin erosion.

Python (Pandas, openpyxl) MySQL (CTEs, STDDEV) ANOVA Principles Excel VBA Dashboard
Business Problem & Scope

Half of 7,991 orders missed the 5-day delivery target ($36.3M exposed). Management prepared to overhaul warehouse NMK1003 due to its high late-order volume.

Methodology & Finding
  • Volume Illusion Caught: NMK1003 generated 31.9% of late orders simply because it handled 31.4% of total order volume.
  • ANOVA Variance Decomposition: Revealed that only 0.08% of delivery-time variance was between facilities; all six sites share an identical 2.82–2.88 day spread.
  • Discount Cliff: Orders with $\ge 20\%$ discount collapsed net margin to 4.64% (vs. 31.02% at 5–10% discount).
Strategic Business Action

Halted planned physical facility renovations, redirecting investment to upstream 3PL carrier dispatch SLAs. Established an executive 15% discount approval limit.

2. Ride Demand Acceleration & Dynamic Revenue Analytics

Separated peak volume from demand acceleration to optimize fleet mobilization, and uncovered confounding variables behind driver rating metrics.

MySQL (`LAG()`, `ROW_NUMBER`) Python (EDA) Power BI (.pbix) DAX Modeling
Business Problem & Scope

Analyzed 35,000 ride-sharing records across 5 metropolitan zones to determine optimal surge pricing timing and fleet tier deployment.

Methodology & Finding
  • Surge Acceleration Identified: Using `LAG()` window queries, proved demand accelerates at 5 PM (+138%), an hour before the 6 PM volume peak.
  • Airport Revenue Concentration: Airport trips comprise 39.6% of volume but generate 54.8% of gross revenue (₹535.77 vs ₹289.40 avg fare).
  • Confounder Caught: Lower-rated drivers appeared to earn more, but controlling for distance revealed fare/km is flat at ₹22/km (ratings dropped due to trip fatigue).
Strategic Business Action

Shifted driver surge incentives to 4:30 PM to mobilize fleet supply ahead of demand. Disproved rating-based driver penalties on long trips.

3. Subscription Retention & Revenue-at-Risk Analytics

Analyzed 7,032 subscriber records to isolate contract confounders and target retention budgets directly toward high-exposure customer cohorts.

Python (Pandas, SciPy) Chi-Square & Cramér's V MySQL Excel Dashboard
Business Problem & Scope

A subscription business faced a 26.6% customer churn rate. Retention spend needed prioritization based on dollar revenue at risk rather than raw customer count.

Methodology & Finding
  • Contract Risk Concentration: Month-to-month contracts churn at 42.7% and account for 72.0% of total revenue-at-risk.
  • Confounder Discovered: Fiber-optic customers exhibited elevated churn, but segmentation proved the driver was contract structure, not network quality.
  • Statistical Testing: Chi-square test (1179.5) and Cramér's V (0.41) confirmed contract tenure is the single primary retention predictor.
Strategic Business Action

Reallocated retention subsidies away from broad campaigns and into a targeted 90-day onboarding offer converting month-to-month users into annual agreements.
